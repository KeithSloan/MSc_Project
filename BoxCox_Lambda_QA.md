# Box-Cox λ and Galaxy Evolution — Q&A

## Question
Does the value of the Box-Cox λ parameter tell us anything about galaxy evolution?

## Answer

Yes. λ increases monotonically from **−0.20 to +2.70** across redshift bins z = 0.002–0.35 (Spearman r = 1.000), tracking the build-up of the red sequence over cosmic time.

### Physical interpretation of λ

| λ value | Interpretation |
|---|---|
| λ < 0 | Distribution sharply bounded on one side — consistent with a consolidated, absorbing red sequence |
| λ → 0 | Log-Normal scatter — multiplicative processes dominating (e.g., mergers, stochastic star formation) |
| λ = 1 | Normal/additive scatter — symmetric, well-mixed colour distribution |
| λ > 1 | Blue cloud dominating — red sequence not yet consolidated |

### Evolutionary narrative

At **higher redshift** (earlier cosmic time), fewer galaxies have quenched. The u−r colour distribution is broad and blue-skewed, giving high λ. As cosmic time progresses, quenching consolidates galaxies onto a tight red sequence, pulling λ negative.

Crucially, **λ < 0 implies an absorbing boundary** — galaxies pile up at the red end and cannot easily return to blue. This maps directly onto the physical picture of quenching as largely irreversible, making λ not just a statistical convenience but a statement about galaxy physics.
